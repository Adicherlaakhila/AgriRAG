# Reflection (≈180 words)

Building AgriRAG surfaced two important failure modes. First, TF-IDF can surface a dosage-bearing tomato note for an unrelated chemical question because shared words like “spray” or “pest” raise similarity. Relying on the LLM prompt alone would still risk invented product names. The fix was a deterministic gate in application code: dosage language is intercepted before Groq, and only an exact crop–pest–approved-guide match (tomato serpentine leaf miner NSKE) may quote a dose. Second, pest symptom keyword overlap is not a diagnosis. Early drafts could have presented the top row as confirmed; the matcher now requires stronger overlap, labels results as possible matches, and escalates ambiguous symptoms to the Agriculture Officer/KVK with fictional placeholders clearly marked.

These checks were found by running the eight automated pipeline tests and by manually probing cotton dosage and nonsense tomato symptoms. Remaining limitations include synthetic weather (never a forecast), a small curated knowledge base, and classroom-wide Supabase anon policies that are unsuitable for real farmer PII. Whenever retrieval is weak, dosage is unverified, or symptoms are ambiguous, the farmer should be referred to the local Agriculture Officer/KVK rather than given speculative advice.
